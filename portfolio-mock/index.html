Lumen — Adaptive Focus Timer

An iOS app that learns a user’s attention rhythm and reshapes work sessions around it, instead of forcing a fixed Pomodoro block.

Problem: rigid timers ignore that focus capacity fluctuates across the day. Research with 12 students surfaced three failure modes in existing apps. I framed the solution as an on-device model that infers session length from interaction signals, then designed and shipped the SwiftUI client end to end. Logical core: predict → suggest → learn from the accept/reject signal.

Artwork / Project image
Craftsmanship-quality screenshots or a hero shot. For video work: scene screenshots plus a link to the online publication.
Impact

Shipped to TestFlight with 40 users; median focused minutes per session rose 23% over two weeks. Led a team of four and owned the model pipeline.

What I learned

Scoping an ML feature to ship on-device taught me to trade model accuracy against latency and privacy — and to defend that trade-off to the team.

OG Studio — Dynamic Image Engine

A self-hosted service that renders branded social preview images on the edge, in under 50 ms.

Problem: hand-made share images don’t scale across hundreds of pages. I researched edge-render constraints, then built a templating layer on Cloudflare Workers that composes typography and art into a cached PNG. The design reasoning centered on cache keys: identical inputs must map to one durable asset.

Artwork / Project image
Show command of technique — before/after, architecture diagram, or a grid of generated outputs.
Impact

Powers preview images across a live portfolio platform; eliminated manual asset work entirely. Open-sourced and used by 3 other developers.

What I learned

Designing for the edge forced discipline around cold starts and binary size — constraints I now treat as first-class design inputs.
